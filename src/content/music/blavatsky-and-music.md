## Introduction

Helena Petrovna Blavatsky (hereafter H.P.B.) was a Russian writer, occultist and theosophist. She was also one of the founders of the Theosophical Society and contributed to the spread of modern theosophy. But there is one aspect of her life about which little has been written and which is by far one of the most interesting: music in the life of H.P.B. The following document gathers information from various sources that mention music in her life and work — from her passages and ventures as a pianist, through her anecdotes with the physical musical world, to the influences of subtle planes that interacted with the reality we know today. Finally, a brief note on her contributions to science, philosophy and occultism in this area.

## Madame Laura

Helena Petrovna Blavatsky, in her many facets, gifts, travels and adventures, had a great deal of contact with music — as artist, as researcher and as esotericist. As an artist not much is known, but there are scattered references identifying her as a pianist of great quality. Henry Olcott himself reports (in his _Old Diary Leaves_, or _The History of the Theosophical Society I_) that she was a student of the great Ignaz Moscheles — himself a pupil of Salieri among others, and teacher of figures of the stature of Felix Mendelssohn. [3] There is information indicating that it is very probable that H.P.B. undertook a concert tour in Russia and Europe during 1872–73, according to Olcott's writings. [1] She did so under the pseudonym Madame Laura. [4] After this period of concerts she is not often mentioned as a pianist; even so, it is known that she played in private and very rarely with accompaniment. Olcott says, literally:

> "...H.P.B. played the piano admirably, with a technique and an expression simply superb. Her hands were models for sculptors — in both the real and the figurative sense — and they were never more beautiful than when flying over the keyboard, drawing magical melodies from it. She was a pupil of Moscheles, and when she was in London with her father, at sixteen years of age, she played at a charity concert with Madame Clara Schumann and Madame Arabella Goddard a piece by Schumann for three pianos..." (Olcott) [4]

And, on more impressive and out-of-the-ordinary occasions:

> "At times, when her body was occupied by one of the Mahatmas, she played with an indescribable power. She would sit at the piano at dusk, sometimes, the two of us alone in the room, and draw from the instrument improvisations that might well make one imagine one was listening to a choir of _gandharvas_, the celestial singers. It was a divine harmony." (Olcott) [4]

There are accounts in which her sister Vera speaks of her musical talents, and indicates that she belonged to the Philharmonic Society in London. [2]

## Harmonies in the air

As a born adventurer and traveller of the world, she had much contact with the musical side of important cultures. One of her journeys reflecting this is recorded in her book _From the Caves and Jungles of Hindostan_. Here it is worth clarifying that it is very probable that music as we know it today throughout the world has Indian origins, as a fakir explains to "the Colonel" in one of their many conversations on the subject in that book:

> "...The music of the ancient Aryans is an antediluvian plant, no doubt, but one most worthy of study and consideration. So it has been thoroughly demonstrated by one of our countrymen, the Rajah Sourindro Mohun Tagore... He is a doctor of music and has no end of decorations from kings and emperors for his book on the music of the Aryans. He has shown, indeed, with the clearest evidence, that ancient India has a perfect right to be called the Mother of Music. So, at least, the best critics of England acknowledge..." (Blavatsky) [5]

In fact, later in that same conversation, she mentions that the purest and highest of all the musical forms of antiquity belongs to India, reaching back into prehistoric times.

> "...In one of the sarcophagi of Thebes, Bruce found a harp of twenty strings, and to judge by such an instrument we may be sure that the earliest inhabitants of Egypt knew the mysteries of harmony; but, with the exception of the Egyptians, we were the only ones to possess this art in remote ages, when the rest of Humanity was still struggling with the elements to wrest from them what was needed for its wretched existence. We possess certain Sanskrit manuscripts on music that have never been translated, not even into the modern Hindu dialects. Some of them are four thousand and eight thousand years old..." (Blavatsky) [5]

And above all, the capacity of the soul to perceive the indefinable sounds of Nature and to be impressed and moved by them. In this connection the book relates an anecdote about a mysterious island that Blavatsky visited together with "the Colonel". There they experienced a musical phenomenon performed by Nature: the stems of a bamboo-like plant are bored by insects of the region which, for no apparent reason, make holes in the stems as if forming giant flutes. At certain hours, in certain positions and specific phases of the moon, a mysterious wind blows with a certain intensity and frequency through these giant natural flutes and produces astonishing harmonies. But it is described more poetically by Blavatsky herself:

> "...Suddenly, amid the solitude and silence of the surroundings, we began to hear quite clearly those same musical notes that moments before had escaped our notice, like a full orchestra beginning to tune its instruments. Here and there were heard the vibrations of violin strings and the high notes of flutes. A gust of wind sprang up shortly and there burst out across the whole island the chords of hundreds of Aeolian horns, thereby beginning the wildest and most sonorous symphony. Through the reaches of the forest an indescribable melody was produced, and between the sad and solemn Aeolian chords there slipped the arpeggios of something like a funeral march, while the tremolo of flutes and clarinets mimicked in the air the cadences of the nightingale. Now they died away as in a long sigh, now they raised their pitch like a hundred silver bells, moving from the gentle tarantella that brings forgetfulness of all human pain to the tearing roar of the beast whose young are taken from her; from human song to the vague arpeggios of the cello, and from the weeping of a child to its seraphic smile... All this chaotic musical assembly grew vast, repeated itself in every direction through the mocking Echo, as if hundreds of fabulous woodland maidens, woken in their palaces of foliage, answered by taking part in that wild saturnalian music..." (Blavatsky) [5]

In India there are many of these natural orchestras, and this kind of reed is known as _vina-devi_ ("the lute of the gods"), [5] and the mysterious animals that make the tiny holes are thousands of minute wood-borers. That astonishing natural mechanism of music is recorded also in far earlier times, even in the Vedas.

Blavatsky continues, in the mouth of the fakir of the anecdote, stressing the importance of appreciating the music of different cultures, bearing in mind that this is sometimes possible only with an ear educated for it. A short fragment taken from the last part of that chapter runs:

> "...there is a great difference between true musical technique, melodies fit for an educated ear, and the ear of one who is not. A musical work may be perfect according to technique; yet its whole may nevertheless be superior to and beyond the reach of an uncultivated taste... what I can assure you of is that our ancestors, like those of the Chinese, were very far from being inferior to modern Europeans — if not in technical instrumentation, then at least in the abstract notions of music..." (Blavatsky) [5]

In India, according to the passage, no sound is permitted in the melodies that cannot be classified among the animate voices of Nature, [5] and music is considered, as they consider themselves, pantheist:

> "...Pantheists in the highest sense, our music, so to speak, is pantheist too, and at the same time highly scientific..." (Blavatsky) [5]

As for the natural musical notes we know today, the book mentions that they come from detailed observation of the sounds produced by certain animals: thus the seven fundamental tones correspond to the respective cries of the peacock, the sheep, the cow, the parrot, the frog, the tiger and the elephant. [5]

> "In this way the musical octave was discovered. As for the subdivisions of the tones and the measure of the notes, the other animals likewise gave them the norms." (Blavatsky)

And she draws this definition from a comparison with Chinese music. Of the latter she mentions that it has eight fundamental sounds serving as the basis of the other derived tones: metal, stone, silk, bamboo, gourd, baked earth, leather and wood. [5] For this reason Chinese music lends itself less to melody than to a series of notes of differing timbre.

> "...The Chinese imperial hymn, for example, is nothing but a set of interminable unisons." (Blavatsky) [5]

And these sounds, notes and music in general are closely related to Mathematics and Astronomy, and hence to the planets, the sun and to numbers generally; they depend on the ether of space, whose existence has not yet been fully proved by modern scientists. This concept — the Music of the Spheres — which Pythagoras introduced into Greece and Italy, really comes from the most ancient Vedas. It is in fact said that this knowledge was given to Pythagoras by the Indian gymnosophists. The Vedas thus reveal much musical knowledge; so much so that one of the four, the _Sama-Veda_, consists only of hymns — a collection of mantrams sung during the sacrifices to the gods.

## Esotericism in music

And so, in many of her works reference is made to music. In _Nightmare Tales_, at least two of the stories are intimately bound up with music: "The Ensouled Violin" and "The Cave of the Echoes". The latter is a narrative of mesmerism with impressive examples of how sounds are spiritual receptacles and transmitters on subtle planes. [6]

In these narratives the importance of music in the rituals of almost every culture is also made visible. The following fragment is taken from "The Cave of the Echoes":

> "Then he who played the drum began a slow, low, solemn and impressive chant...
>
> ...Another chant, another roll of the drum, and the whole mountain shook to its foundations, with crashes like formidable cannon fired in the endless, dark corridors..." (Blavatsky)

As with this example, that esoteric side of music is present in many of Blavatsky's writings and even in personal anecdotes. One of these, for example, is Olcott's account that on occasions when there were visitors in the house, bells of different tones mysteriously sounded with no apparent origin. Some time later it became known that Blavatsky produced them voluntarily in the air. When Olcott asked her about it, she explained that it is difficult to explain, just as it is difficult to explain ordinary whistling: a born whistler cannot explain exactly the technique of whistling nor how he acquired it; simply, having discovered it, he can do it without further theory. This is how many phenomena were produced in the most natural way by H.P.B.

> "...I think a note; instinctively or automatically, I act by my will upon the astral current. I send from my brain towards a given point in space a kind of counter-current. Something like a whirlpool forms at the point where my current meets the great wave of astral light that follows the movement of the Earth, and the note I think sounds in that eddy. Exactly as the note you want resounds in the vacuum formed by your lips when you place them as required, along with the muscles of your throat, to force the air through that opening. I cannot explain it to you in any other way. It is something I can do, but I cannot tell you how I do it." (Blavatsky) [3]

But the mystery of music is explored further by Blavatsky in one of her most celebrated and profound works of occultism, _Isis Unveiled_. One aspect of music explored in this marvellous book is the singular influence of music on certain illnesses, above all nervous ones. And this is something that was without doubt already known in remote times — by the Egyptians, through the biblical passages of harps, in India, down to the recent scientific experiments of Kircher and Mesmer and to contemporary music therapy. [7]

> "Musical sounds have a property of attraction which expels and carries away in its vibrations the ailment. Twenty centuries ago Asclepiades already made use of the sound of a trumpet to cure sciatica, whose pain ceased through the vibration of the nerve fibres. Analogously Democritus affirms that many illnesses are cured to the sound of the flute, and Mesmer employed in his magnetic cures the drum of Kircher." (Blavatsky)

And later, in the same _Isis Unveiled_, she draws a correspondence between music and other aspects of nature through their relation as waves, since sound (like other phenomena such as light, smell, forms and movement) has vibratory properties: [8]

> "Just as the planets differ in magnitude, distance and movement, so too is there a difference not only in the impulse each communicates to the ether or astral light, but also in the subtle forces they radiate according to their position in space. Music is a modulated combination of sounds, and sound is etheric vibration in the air." (Blavatsky)

For this reason, Blavatsky explains, since we too possess vibratory properties, everything else that vibrates in the Universe — music among it — strikes and conditions us, depending also on our capacities of concentration and will: [8]

> "...certain kinds of music make us frantic, while others swell our soul with religious fervour. There is scarcely a human creation that does not respond to particular vibrations of the atmosphere. The same happens with colours: some excite us and others calm us." (Blavatsky)

It is for this reason that it is proven that even in animals music produces astonishing effects, such as those H.P.B. relates from her journey to Egypt about the relaxing reaction that the sweet sound of the flute produces in reptiles. [9] On this point, in the same _Isis Unveiled_, there are very clear and profound references to Egypt. The Egyptians came to know musical harmony and, in general, to master to a high degree the vibratory waves of nature. The Egyptian priests sang in honour of Serapis a hymn composed of the seven vowels, and to the sound of the seventh vowel and the seventh ray of the rising sun the statue of Memnon responded. [10]

> "With this coincides the emerging discovery of the marvellous properties of the violet ray, the seventh of the prismatic spectrum, which surpasses all in chemical potency and corresponds to the seventh note of the musical scale." (Blavatsky)

H.P.B. also mentions that the Egyptians knew the secrets of harmony and its influence on the spirit, for which reason they set aside sanatoria in the temples where music was employed for healing. [10]

> "In the carvings and reliefs of the most ancient periods of Egypt one sees musicians playing various instruments, as well as orchestral groups whose director beats time with his hand. This shows that they knew the laws of harmony." (Blavatsky)

Later she mentions that the Egyptians encompassed in their music three principal genres: religious, civic and military. And they already knew instruments such as the lyre, harp, flute, guitar, bagpipes and drums, among others. It is also said that Pythagoras learned music in Egypt, in order to establish in Greece the methodical study of this art, whose most notable professors were Egyptians, since they knew the combination of strings and the multiplicity of tonalities determined by their length. [10]

Blavatsky was an inspiration to later esotericists who produced work related to music, inspired by her treatises. One of these is entitled "Occult or Exact Science?", published in _The Theosophist_, [11] in which she sets out examples of synaesthesia (the capacity to have effects in one sense produced by another). One of them is that of a girl who perceived colours in people's words: [11]

> "'Mamma, I love you very much. Today you are good and affectionate with me. Your words are entirely blue.' 'What do you mean?' asked the mother. 'Your words are all blue, because they are very affectionate; but when you scold me they appear red — but so red! It is worse still when you get angry with papa, because then they are orange... horrible...'" (Blavatsky)

In another case she relates the anecdote of a man whose case was the inverse of the previous one: this person "heard" the sound of objects, such as a flower, an aroma, the green, red and blue sounds:

> "My hearing," he writes, "acquired marvellous capacities: I heard the music of flowers; the green, red and blue sounds penetrated my ear very perceptibly, in the manner of waves of aroma and colour. A falling glass, the creak of a chair, a word murmured in the lowest tone, vibrated and resounded within me like thunder. At the slightest contact with objects — furniture, or human bodies — I heard prolonged sounds, sighs like the melodious vibrations of an Aeolian harp." (_La Presse_, 10 July 1840)

H.P.B. then extends the analysis, mentioning that these astral capacities have already been corroborated and recorded by some recent researchers, of which perhaps the most formal document is found in an issue of _La Nature_ (no. 626, 1885, p. 406 et seq.), in an article by A. de Rochas, who experimented with a gentleman who saw colours when he heard sounds. The study was so thorough that correspondences are made of vowels with colours, syllables with chords, and so on. The sound each instrument played also had a particular shade in the combination of colours: thus the piano and the flute suggested bluish tints, the violin blacks, the guitar was silver-grey, and so on. [12]

The point H.P.B. stresses and reminds us of again in this passage is without doubt that this knowledge is millennia old, and that today we are only just uncovering it once more, little by little. A student of esotericism knows, after studying the evolution of the races, the evolutionary order of the senses in each race and sub-race, in which it is known that the first senses developed by humanity were touch and hearing, then sight, while the rest remained latent. That is why sight was associated in its evolution with sounds. [12]

This is corroborated too in the millennia-old wisdom of the Vedas: [12]

> "...in them the concepts of the words sound and light, to hear and to see, are always associated. In Hymn X, 71, verse 4, we read: 'Though one look he does not see the speech, and another who sees does not hear it.' Also in verse 7, in which a party of friends is presented competing in song, they are characterised with two epithets, one following the other: _Akshavanta_ and _Karnavanta_, or 'one endowed with eyes' and 'one endowed with ears'. The latter is natural: the singer has a good ear for music, and the epithet is understandable in view of the musical emulation. But what sense can _Akshavanta_ with his good sight have in this case, unless there is in it a relation and a significance that are not explained because the hymn probably refers to days when sight and hearing were synonymous terms?" (Blavatsky)

On the other hand, the Sanskrit verbal root _Arc_ is used to give two meanings: (a) to sing, and (b) to shine, to cast rays of light. The nouns _rc_ and _arka_, derived from the root _Arc_, are used to mean first song, hymn, and second brightness, ray, sun. [12]

Among many other explanatory articles, one published in _The Theosophist_ (Vol. III, No. 11) stands out, in which she analyses the sevenfold character of various domains and relates them to one another. The following table is taken and freely translated from that article:

| Principle | Colour | Note | Number | State of matter |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Chhaya, Shadow or Double | Violet | B | 1 | Ether |
| Higher Manas, Higher Intelligence | Indigo | A | 2 | Critical state, air |
| Auric envelope | Blue | G | 3 | Smoke or vapour |
| Lower Manas, or Animal Soul | Green | F | 4 | Critical state |
| Buddhi, or Spiritual Soul | Yellow | E | 5 | Water |
| Prana, principle of life | Orange | D | 6 | Critical state |

## Conclusions

Helena Petrovna Blavatsky was a great philosopher who left us a great legacy: the understanding of nature through the knowledge of ourselves and of the laws that govern the Universe. In this sense her investigations of music, of sound and of the elements that vibrate with one another and their relation among themselves represent a major contribution. H.P.B. possessed, among her many qualities, a broad taste for music — not only as a philosopher but also as an artist.
